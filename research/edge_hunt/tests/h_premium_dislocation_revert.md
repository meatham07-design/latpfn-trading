## Interpretation

**Headline (walk-forward OOS, perp-led flush long, maker):** n=7, net=-209.7 USD (-300 bps/trade), PF 0.19, folds 2+3 = [-97.3, -112.4], shuffle p=0.955, neighbours 1/4 positive. Verdict: **none**.

1. **The signal barely exists.** Over 208 days x 124 coins (120k eligible coin-hours) there are 425 flush bars (<= -2.5 ATR24), but only 18 with a bar-end premium <= -20 bps and 14 with a premium change <= -20 bps. The median premium move on a flush bar is -0.75 bps (10th pct -5 bps), and the fraction of flushes that reach -10 bps relative to the coin's own 7-day median is 5%. HL's hourly premium stamp does not register 1h flushes as mark/oracle dislocations, so "perp-led" as defined here cannot be identified from this series at the hourly resolution. The absolute-level condition is additionally contaminated by six names with a structural premium of -20 to -110 bps (AZTEC, BLUR, ACE, AXS, BERA, STABLE); the `rel` mode removes that and changes nothing.
2. **When the premium IS dislocated on a flush, the flush continues, it does not revert.** Descriptive forward returns (no trading rules): flush bars with premium <= -20 bps -> +131/-97/-209 bps (fold 1), -288/-308/-436 (fold 2), -645/-87/-879 (fold 3) at 6/12/24h; flush bars with premium > -20 bps -> +10..+40 / +17..+71 / -102..+118. Same picture with the relative premium (<= -30: -115/-147/-395 bps; -10..-5: +93/+112/+74). The hypothesis is inverted in every fold: the premium dislocation marks the flushes that keep going (oracle lagging a real repricing, or a liquidation cascade still in progress), not the ones that snap back. The premium-only cell (dislocation without a flush, 98 OOS trades) loses -2860 USD (-292 bps/trade), consistent with round 1's basis fade.
3. **The continuation arm (short the dislocated flush) is the right sign but has no sample:** OOS n=2 (level grid) / n=13 (relative grid, net +0.6). Nothing can be concluded at this n.
4. **The comparison cells are the interesting result, and they are the opposite conditioning.** With identical mechanics, `spot_led` (flush, premium NOT dislocated) is OOS n=272 net=+397 PF 1.29 (rel grid: n=262 net=+503 PF 1.47), shuffle p=0.005, 4/4 neighbours positive, 38/64 coins positive, but fold 3 is -39 (rel: +32), the PIT-extension names lose (-258), and the hour-level t-stat is only 0.82 (108 signal hours). `market_wide` (>= 5 coins flushing in the same hour) is OOS n=149 net=+540 PF 2.53, folds +207/+333, shuffle p=0.005, robust to taker fees (+496) and to removing the best hour (+274) - but it is **12 signal hours** in the OOS period (7 of 12 positive, hour-level t=1.73), so the trade-level Sharpe of 3.4 is an artefact of clustering. Because the premium is almost never dislocated on these bars, the normalisation exit fires at the first bar end: the effective strategy in both cells is "maker bid at the flush close, sell at the open of t+2" (avg hold 2.3-2.7h, 264/272 and 148/149 exits via `norm`). This is a 1-2 bar fade, a horizon that neither round-1 nor round-2 flush tests used (they held 12-24h and lost the reversal to trends).
5. **Execution caveats for the positive cells:** maker fill at the flush close requires bar t+1 to trade through it, which is verified here with 1 tick, but in a live cascade the queue at that price is long and a partial/no fill in the best hours is likely; fills are assumed for the full $1000 notional. The market-wide hours are exactly the hours when HL is most stressed.

**Bottom line:** the hypothesised discriminator (HL premium) is not informative for reversal - it is a weak continuation flag with ~15 events per 200 days. The reversal that exists is in flushes with a *quiet* premium, especially when many coins flush together, at a 1-2 hour horizon.
